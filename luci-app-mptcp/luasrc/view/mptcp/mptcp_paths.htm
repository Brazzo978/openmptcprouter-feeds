<%:Legend and reading guide%>
  • <%:Path saturation%>: <%:0-100 pressure estimate. It is driven mainly by RTT growth over the observed baseline and TCP send queue. This is the value to watch when a line starts saturating.%>
  • <%:RTT baseline / delta%>: <%:baseline is the lowest RTT observed for that path during the current page session; delta is the current increase over that baseline.%>
  • <%:WAN load share%>: <%:physical RX+TX traffic share from interface counters. It tells which line is carrying more traffic, but not whether that line is saturated.%>
  • <%:RX/TX imbalance%>: <%:deviation from an even split, in percentage points. With two active WANs, 0 pp means 50/50; +10 pp means about 60/40 on that direction.%>
  • <%:Vs recent peak%>: <%:current physical rate compared with the highest rate seen on the same path since opening or resetting the graph.%>
  • <%:MPTCP share%>: <%:estimated from visible MPTCP subflow byte counters in ss. Useful for scheduler behavior, less authoritative than physical WAN counters.%>
  • <%:Queue%>: <%:TCP send/receive queue size. A growing send queue together with rising RTT is a strong saturation signal.%>
  • <%:cwnd / windows%>: <%:cwnd is the congestion window; snd_wnd and rcv_wnd are the advertised TCP send/receive windows. rcv_space and rcv_ssthresh show receive-side room and threshold.%>
  • <%:BBR / rates%>: <%:BBR bw is the congestion-control bandwidth estimate; delivery, pacing and send are kernel TCP rate hints for the same subflow.%>
  • <%:loss / retrans%>: <%:kernel TCP loss and retransmission counters when exposed by ss. Treat them as diagnostic events, not as a perfect physical WAN packet-loss percentage.%>
  • <%:segments%>: <%:segs and data show TCP segment counters out/in. They help distinguish real data movement from keepalive or control traffic.%>
  • <%:Idle filter%>: <%:traffic below 200 kbit/s is ignored for active/share calculations to hide SSH, keepalives and counter noise.%>
